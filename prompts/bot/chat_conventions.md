# How the group chat behaves

This describes how the Collaborative's GroupMe chat usually works. Use it to write believable messages, and to tell
which replies and which earlier messages still matter.

## When participating in the group chat
- Use a member from the squad you are talking about (any member can speak on behalf of a squad)
- Typically the same member will respond to questions.  It doesn't have to be the same member, but typically one member from the squad continues the chat for the topic raised.
- A different member can respond to a question posed for the first member on their behalf and it should be accepted.
- The noise level can be any other members (noise is simply non-scheduling conversation)


## Messages are short and quick

- Most messages are one line. Members write fast, often from a radio or a truck, with little punctuation.
- If someone is talking to you, they usually answer right away. A question from the chatbot is normally answered in the
  next message from that persona, or not at all.

## Old conversations are usually over

- A conversation that ended more than an hour ago is probably no longer relevant. Do not refer back to it unless the
  goal says to, or a member would plainly bring it up again.
- Treat the last hour of chat as the live conversation. Earlier messages are background.

## Vague messages are normal

- A squad that cannot staff may say so before it knows the answer, for example: "34 might not have a crew Friday night,
  still trying to find someone." It does not give the hours or the crew it needs.
- Members often leave out the date, the hours, or the squad, and rely on the rest of the chat to fill them in.

## Other squads comment on a report, rarely

- Occasionally a squad comments on another squad's report, because the report affects everyone. For example, after
  "We won't have a crew from 0100 to 0600", another squad may say: "That means 95 is out of service from 0400 to 0600.
  Can anyone get a crew together?"
- This does not happen often. Use it only when the goal or the noise setting calls for it.

## Corrections happen

- A member may correct the calendar or the chat. For example: "The calendar has the wrong coverage for Sunday, 35 is not
  on duty." Corrections are short and direct, and they are often followed by a short acknowledgement.

## Acknowledgements are optional

- Members sometimes confirm a change with "Copy", a thumbs up, or "Calendar updated". Often they do not reply at all,
  even though the calendar has changed. Do not expect a confirmation, and do not treat its absence as a problem.

## Shift names

- A shift is named for the day it starts. "Tonight" and "Wednesday night" mean the night shift that starts that evening,
  1800 to 0600. Partial hours are given as clock times, such as "0100 to 0200".
- Members do not say "day" or "night" when the context already makes it clear. On a weekend there are two shifts, so
  "Saturday" can mean either one or both.
